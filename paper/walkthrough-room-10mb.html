From step zero: how the mesh grows to 10 MB

Scene room (indoor preset), requested size 10 MB. Numbers marked (log) are from the real run. Numbers marked (est.) are plausible fill-ins so every line of arithmetic can be followed.

1. The two kinds of quantities

Everything on this page is one of two kinds. Keeping them apart is the whole trick.

KindExamplesLives where
Accumulated over an interval (an interval = the 500 training steps between two boundaries)the error ledger ΔD_t, and therefore ratio_tBuilt up during the interval, read once at its end, then wiped
Set at a boundary and held constant through the next intervalλ, σ, yield, the byte-table prices b_v / overhead, V*Rewritten at a boundary, used unchanged until the next one

There is one variable λ. It is rewritten at every boundary. The λ that is used at a boundary is the one written at the previous boundary.

2. Step 0: what exists before any training

  • Photos of the room go through COLMAP: camera poses plus a sparse point cloud (a few hundred thousand points, each with a colour).
  • For every point, one triangle: centred on the point, size 2.23 × the distance to the nearest other point, random orientation, the point's colour, opacity 0.1. Each triangle owns its own three vertices; nothing is shared. This is the "soup".
  • The error ledger: one row per triangle, all zero.
  • λ₁ = 5.2 × 10⁻³ (log), from a one-line guess: λ₁ = 6.2e-3 · (9 / target_MB)^1.67. It is used exactly once, at the first boundary, and never again.
  • Byte table at its priors, used until the first measurement: b_v 6 B, b_f 2 B, fpv 2.5, overhead 0.6 MB. σ = 1 (nothing gets pruned before step 3000, so every new vertex survives). yield = 1 (nothing measured yet).

3. Interval 1: steps 0 → 1500, and the first boundary

── steps 0 → 1500: training. Every step: ──────────────────────────────
    render one training photo with the current triangles
    error per pixel = |render − photo|
    add each pixel's error to the row of the triangle that painted it     ← ledger 1 fills
    Adam moves vertices, colours, opacities                            ← upstream, unchanged
    (no pruning before 3000, no cutting before 1500: pure optimisation)

── boundary 1500 (the first decision) ───────────────────────────────────
1  (pruning is still off)                      V = 3 × #points ≈ 450 000 (est.)
2  read ledger 1:   ratio¹_t = ΔD_t / 33 B   for every triangle
3  V* = (aim − overhead) / (b_v + fpv·b_f) = (12.5 − 0.6) MB / (6 + 5) B   → V* ≈ 1 082 000 (priors)
4  remaining = 17 boundaries (1500, 2000, …, 9500), σ = 1 for now
       f = (V*/V)^(1/17) = (1 082 000 / 450 000)^(1/17)                      → f = 1.053
       desired = V·(f − 1)/(3·σ) = 450 000 × 0.053 / 3                      → ≈ 8 000 (est.)
5  cap = min(upstream limit 0.27·V = 121 500, desired)                      → 8 000
6  cut triangles with ratio¹_t > λ₁ (5.2e-3), at most 8 000 of them
7  write λ for the next boundary:
       V_exp = V + σ·(added) ; desired_next = line 4 again with remaining = 16
       k_asked = desired_next / yield(=1)
       λ₂ = the k_asked-th largest value in ratio¹                        → held constant until 2000
8  wipe ledger 1, start ledger 2 with the new triangle count
Notice the order inside the boundary: the λ that selects the cut (line 6) is λ₁, written before this interval. The λ written here (line 7) is λ₂, and nothing is done with it until boundary 2000. There is no step at which a λ is written and used on the same ledger.

4. Interval 2: steps 1500 → 2000, second boundary

── steps 1500 → 2000: training, ledger 2 fills (the 8 000 × 4 new children have rows too) ──
── boundary 2000 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
2  ratio²_t = ledger 2 / 33 B
3  V* unchanged (no new measurement yet)
4  remaining = 16; f = (V*/V)^(1/16); desired = …
6  cut ratio²_t > λ₂, at most cap
   count how many passed: n_pass. yield := n_pass / k_asked (the first real yield)
7  λ₃ = k_asked-th largest of ratio², with k_asked = desired_next / yield
8  wipe ledger 2

6. Why the line is written one boundary early, instead of sorting and taking the top k now

Taking the top 85 350 of ratio¹⁷ directly would work as a counter. We keep the rule cut if ratio_t > λ with λ fixed before the ledger it is applied to, because that is exactly the rule of the fixed-λ method; the only difference is that λ is now rewritten every boundary from the scene's own numbers. It also keeps λ meaningful as a price: if the scene has fewer triangles worth cutting than planned, fewer pass and λ is pushed down to its floor, and the run reports "this scene cannot buy more quality" instead of forcing cuts. The cost is that the chart shrinks a little during the 500 steps (training lowers error), so fewer than k pass; that is the yield, measured and divided back in.

8. After growth: the same relay, run backwards (12 500 → 19 500)

At 11 000 upstream stitches the soup into a shared-vertex mesh; from then on nothing can add vertices. Every 500 steps from 12 500 to 19 500 the boundary block is:

1  probe the mesh with the real encoder (all four byte-table values now measured)   → "delivered today" MB
2  that reading drifts upward until 24 000; extend the trend                       → landing MB
3  excess = landing − target ;  one collapse frees b_v + 2·b_f ≈ 9.5 B
   pairs this round = excess / 9.5 B / rounds left
4  if landing ≤ target: nothing this round
5  score every edge: score_uv = ΔD_uv / ΔR_uv      (ledger of this interval, same λ)
       ΔD_uv = bills of all faces around u and v ;  ΔR_uv = bytes the file actually loses
6  merge the lowest scores first, up to the pairs wanted; position, colour, opacity
   and Adam momentum averaged into the surviving vertex
7  record bytes removed, for the next trend
growth: cut t if ΔD_t / ΔR_t > λ     trimming: merge (u,v) if ΔD_uv / ΔR_uv < λ

room 10 MB (log): first probe at 12 500 reads 8.79 MB; the landing estimate rises 8.5 → 9.9 by 16 000 with no edit; 7 rounds from 16 500 merge 67 765 pairs; delivered 10.29 MB, 1.029 × target, 0.03 dB from the fixed-λ curve at equal bytes.

9. One sentence

The user gives megabytes. At every boundary the real encoder prices a vertex, the byte table turns the budget into a vertex count, the plan turns that into "cut k now", and λ for the next boundary is drawn at the k-th bar of this boundary's chart. After growth ends, the same λ is read backwards to merge what the 25 % margin over-bought.
