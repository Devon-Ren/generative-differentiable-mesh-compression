\section{Conclusion}
Opaque differentiable meshes cannot be shrunk after training, so \BM decides their size while they grow: it measures the encoded size during training, plans growth toward the vertex count the target can hold, splits where the rendering error is and trims by edge collapse with training still running; a codec with a standalone decoder writes a standard mesh. The file lands within about 10\% of the requested size on four datasets, 31--122$\times$ smaller than the trained model, in one training run. The remaining gap to Gaussian codecs is the representation's; closing it is the next step.
