# Why MeshSplatting (why a differentiable opaque mesh at all) — answer to the advisor, 2026-10-20

Advisor's question (10/20): "if MeshSplatting itself is not doing good enough in PSNR compared to 3DGS,
why still focus on it? why do people use it? what is the advantage that forces choosing its differentiable
mesh as a representation, unless we prove it to be a better representation in some way?"

## 0. One-paragraph answer

Nobody chooses MeshSplatting for PSNR. People choose it because its output is a **standard opaque
triangle mesh** — the one representation every game engine, browser, phone, physics engine, CAD/DCC tool and
AR pipeline already consumes, with hardware rasterization, depth-buffer occlusion, no per-frame sorting, and
free compatibility with collision, editing, relighting-style baking and LOD. 3DGS and every 3DGS compressor
(HAC++ etc.) produce a semi-transparent point cloud that needs a custom sorted-splat renderer and gives no
surface. The 2.5-3.5 dB cost is paid by the **whole opaque-mesh class** (BakedSDF, MobileNeRF, Binary Opacity
Grids, MiLo, MeshSplatting all sit 2.5-3.5 dB under 3DGS on Mip-360); it is the price of the surface
constraint, not a MeshSplatting defect. Among that class MeshSplatting is the direct, end-to-end
differentiable one (no SDF, no marching cubes, no baking stage, ~1 h training), which makes it the natural
object to compress. What our paper can **prove** is a "better representation in some way": per byte, it is the
best engine-ready mesh by 10-50x, and its redundancy is so structured that a 36-80x codec exists.

## 1. Why people use an opaque mesh at all (deployment facts, not PSNR)

| property | opaque mesh (MeshSplatting output) | 3DGS / compressed 3DGS |
|---|---|---|
| renderer | any rasterizer (Unity/Unreal/three.js/WebGL/mobile GPU) | custom CUDA/WebGL splatting with per-frame depth sort |
| occlusion / depth | z-buffer, exact; composes with other scene content | approximate, alpha-blended; composition with meshes is hard |
| physics / collision / navigation | directly (surface exists) | none (no surface) |
| editing / asset tools / LOD / simplification | standard DCC pipeline | bespoke |
| AR occlusion, shadows, relighting-style baking | standard | research-level |
| frame cost | ~ triangles rasterized, FPS in the hundreds+ on desktop (BOG 927, MobileNeRF 296, BakedSDF 72 in a laptop browser) | depends on sort + overdraw |
| storage | vertices + faces + colour attribute (what we compress) | millions of Gaussians x 59 floats |

These are the reasons MobileNeRF, BakedSDF, BOG, MiLo, SuGaR, 2DGS/GOF/RaDe-GS mesh extraction and
MeshSplatting all exist as a research line despite losing PSNR to volumetric methods. The MeshSplatting
paper itself motivates with exactly this (CVPR 2026, arXiv 2512.06818).

## 2. Why the PSNR gap is a class gap, not a MeshSplatting weakness (numbers we already have)

Mip-360 numbers from analysis/baselines/baselines-room-garden-v1.md:

| method | type | Mip-360 PSNR | asset size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3DGS | volumetric | avg 27.21 (room 31.7) | 350-1300 MB/scene |
| HAC++ | volumetric, compressed | room 31.75 | 3.4 MB |
| Radiance Meshes | volumetric tets (NOT opaque) | indoor 30.61 | not reported |
| Binary Opacity Grids | opaque mesh, baked | indoor 27.71 / outdoor 23.94 | 0.85 GiB |
| BakedSDF | opaque mesh, baked | indoor 27.06 / outdoor 22.47 | 434 MB |
| MobileNeRF | opaque mesh, textures+MLP | garden 23.54 | 239 MB (garden) |
| MiLo | opaque mesh from GS | avg 24.09 | 60-400 MB |
| MeshSplatting | opaque mesh, end-to-end differentiable | avg 24.78 (room 28.26 ours) | ~740 MB FP32; authors' PLY ~100 MB RGB-only (-2 dB) |
| **ours** (MeshSplatting + codec) | opaque mesh | avg -0.26 dB (λ2e-3) / -0.88 dB (λ6e-3) vs MeshSplatting | **19.6 MB / 8.8 MB** mean |

Every opaque-mesh row is 2.5-3.5 dB below 3DGS. Our own diagnostics (quality-gap-assessment-2026-10-19.md:
error anatomy, env-map probe, exposure UB) found no mismatch to fix; the residual is spread over edges and
high-frequency texture, which is what an opaque Gouraud surface cannot represent. So "MeshSplatting is not
good enough in PSNR" is true of the entire class the user base has already accepted.

## 3. Why MeshSplatting specifically within the class

- **End-to-end differentiable**: vertices, connectivity (via splatting of triangles), and per-vertex SH are
  optimized directly against images. BakedSDF/BOG/MobileNeRF train a volumetric field first then bake,
  which is slow (hours to a day), multi-stage, and loses quality at the bake. MiLo/SuGaR extract a mesh from
  Gaussians, then refine; the mesh is a by-product.
- **Best quality in the class at the smallest triangle count**: MeshSplatting 24.78 avg with ~3M vertices vs
  RaDe-GS/GOF meshes 23.56/20.78 at 31-33M vertices, BOG 10M faces (MeshSplatting Table 1).
- **Training ~1 h on one GPU**, no SDF, no marching cubes, no texture atlas.
- **The compression question is only well-posed here**: because geometry and appearance are jointly
  differentiable, rate can be put inside the growth loop (our rate-controlled growth), which is impossible for
  a baked pipeline. That is the generative-compression contribution and it needs a differentiable mesh.

## 4. What we can PROVE as "a better representation in some way"

Provable with our data (no new method needed):

1. **Quality per byte among engine-ready meshes.** At 9-20 MB per scene we match or beat every published
   opaque-mesh method that is 240-850 MB (10-50x smaller). Garden: ours 24.37 at 27 MB (old) / better at
   λ points vs MobileNeRF 23.54 at 239 MB. Indoor: room 27.99 at 13.7 MB vs BOG indoor avg 27.71 at
   0.85 GiB, BakedSDF 27.06 at 434 MB. (Caveat to state: theirs are 4-scene averages; batch A/B give us all
   9 + T&T so the comparison becomes like-for-like.)
2. **Structured redundancy.** 740 MB FP32 carries ~10-25 MB of information; per-vertex SH collapses to a
   shared codebook at -0.1 dB, XYZ needs 14-16 bit, faces are ~2 bit/face after reorder. This is the
   "texture-mapped mesh in disguise" claim (story-v3) and is what makes the representation *compressible
   where 3DGS is not* at equal quality-loss: HAC++ needs a learned context model and still has no surface.
3. **Rate inside the growth loop beats post-hoc reduction** (+0.3..+4.3 dB at equal vertex count on 7/7
   scenes; scenes7-budget-vs-collapse). Only possible because the representation is differentiable.
4. **Deployment**: decoded asset renders in a plain rasterizer at hundreds of FPS (batch C measurement,
   minutes of GPU) and loads in three.js/Unity unchanged.

Not provable and not claimed: higher PSNR than 3DGS; higher PSNR than Radiance Meshes (volumetric).

## 5. Paper changes this implies

- Intro paragraph 1 = "why a mesh" (table in §1 condensed to two sentences + citations).
- Table 2 (cross-representation): add columns **standard mesh? / renderer / FPS (plain rasterizer) /
  training time / size**. Put 3DGS + HAC++ as reference rows, greyed, with the note "no surface".
- Batch C: FPS of decoded meshes in a plain OpenGL/three.js viewer vs triangle count (~0.5 GPU-h).
- One sentence in Limitations: PSNR ceiling is the opaque-surface class ceiling; closing it needs
  volumetric primitives (Radiance Meshes) and loses the deployment properties.

## 6. Suggested reply to the advisor (English, short)

"MeshSplatting is not chosen for PSNR; the whole opaque-mesh family (BakedSDF, MobileNeRF, BOG, MiLo,
MeshSplatting) sits 2.5-3.5 dB under 3DGS because an opaque surface cannot represent what a volume can. People
use these methods because the output is a standard triangle mesh that renders in any engine or browser with a
z-buffer, composes with other content, and supports physics, editing and AR. MeshSplatting is the end-to-end
differentiable member of that family (no SDF/baking, 1 h training, best quality per triangle). What we prove
is that it is the better representation *per byte* in that family: 9-20 MB per scene vs 240-850 MB for the
others at equal or higher PSNR, with a 36-80x codec that only works because the representation is
differentiable and its redundancy is structured."
